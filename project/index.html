Choosing the right guide can make the difference between an empty line and a trophy catch

Choosing the right lure can make the difference between an empty line and a trophy catch, since different lures are built to mimic different prey and trigger strikes from specific species.

What lure should I use? Well, allow me to answer.

Lures and what they catch

Crankbaits

Mimic baitfish, float/dive/hover; versatile across many species.

Spinner baits

Flash and vibration draw bass, pike, muskie, perch.

Spoons

Wobbling action great for fast predators like mackerel and barracuda.

Jigs

Most versatile lure; works bottom to surface, bass to tarpon.

Inline spinners

Built for trout, also strong on bass, pike, and muskie.

Trolling lures

Standard for big offshore fish (marlin, tuna, mahi-mahi).

Core equipment

Lines

Monofilament (stretchy, beginner-friendly), braided (thin, strong), fluorocarbon (near-invisible leader).

Reels

Spinning reels are the easy, versatile beginner choice.

Basic kits

Rod and reel, line/leader, lures, hooks, sinkers, bobbers, tackle box, license.

Offshore gear

Heavier rods/reels, trolling lures, GPS/fish finders, bigger boats.

Land shore vs. deep sea

Species by location

  • Inshore/shore: redfish, snook, trout, flounder, tarpon, striped bass, snapper
  • Deep sea (trolling): marlin, sailfish, swordfish, tuna, mahi-mahi, wahoo
  • Deep sea (bottom): grouper, amberjack, flounder
  • Freshwater: bass, pike, muskie, walleye, trout, perch, crappie
